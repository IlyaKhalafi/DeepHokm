I need to get a MaskablePPO (sb3-contrib) self-play RL agent for Hokm (a 4-player imperfect-information trick-taking card game, teams of 2 opposite each other, 13 tricks/hand, match to 7 hand-wins) to reliably beat a fixed scripted baseline policy, called GreedyPolicy, at an 80%+ win rate in head-to-head evaluation (both seats of one team run the RL policy, both seats of the other team run GreedyPolicy, 100+ game samples). GreedyPolicy is deterministic and has no lookahead: it calls trump for its longest suit, plays the cheapest card that wins a trick if not already winning, and discards its lowest card otherwise. Every GreedyPolicy instance plays identically given the same information (it is a pure function of the observation, no internal randomness).

Network: a small transformer feature extractor (d_model=128, 3 layers, <2M params total) tokenizing hand/trick/history/context, feeding a MaskableActorCriticPolicy (policy head + value head), trained with PPO over a masked Discrete(56) action space.

What has been tried so far this project, all evaluated by playing 100-game (or more) gauntlets:

1. Pure self-play from random initialization, gamma=0.997, sparse +/-1 match reward: never learned (unrelated bug, since fixed -- an opponent snapshot pool was accidentally empty for the whole run due to a fork-timing bug).
2. Fixed run: self-play with per-episode team-coherent opponent draws (latest snapshot / pool / GreedyPolicy / random at various percentages tried: 60/30/0/10, 45/20/25/10, 55/25/10/10), corrected gamma=0.997, hand-level reward shaping (+/-0.15 per hand won/lost on top of the sparse match reward), PPO tuned to avoid a KL blowup (n_epochs=4, batch_size=1024, target_kl=0.02). Across multiple 8M-step runs at different GreedyPolicy training-shares, win rate vs. random reaches 0.55-0.97 depending on the run, but win rate vs. GreedyPolicy specifically plateaus in a 0.29-0.55 band (mean ~0.42) and shows NO trend up or down over 8M steps of training (linear regression slope over 33 evaluation rounds spanning the whole run is ~0.0025 per million steps -- effectively flat). This holds whether GreedyPolicy is 0%, 10%, or 25% of the self-play training opponent mix.
3. Behavioral cloning warm start: collected ~1.7M (observation, action) pairs from GreedyPolicy-vs-GreedyPolicy-vs-GreedyPolicy-vs-GreedyPolicy self-play (every seat an independent GreedyPolicy instance), trained a fresh MaskablePPO policy via supervised cross-entropy to imitate those actions, reaching 99.83% held-out action-prediction accuracy (near-perfect imitation of GreedyPolicy's own decision rule). Loaded this as the initial weights for the SAME 8M-step self-play PPO recipe as #2 above (GreedyPolicy at 10% of the training mix). Result: win rate vs. random jumped enormously (0.88-0.97 by the end, vs. 0.55-0.70 without the warm start), but win rate vs. GreedyPolicy specifically was STILL only in the same 0.29-0.55 band, flat across the whole run (mean 0.42) -- the BC warm start did not move this number at all despite the network being able to represent GreedyPolicy's exact decision rule almost perfectly at initialization.
4. A test-time search enhancement (root-sampled imperfect-information Monte Carlo: sample plausible hidden hands consistent with public play, evaluate the policy's top candidate actions by simulating forward with the same network) was built and evaluated at ~70 paired games: roughly break-even to slightly worse than the plain policy against GreedyPolicy (0.44 win rate). Ruled out as a fix (separate effort, working as designed, just not helping here).

The single most striking fact: a policy that can imitate GreedyPolicy's decision rule almost losslessly (99.83% accuracy) still only wins ~42% of games against it after extensive further RL self-play training, and RL provides no measurable improvement on this specific matchup over 8M steps even when GreedyPolicy is directly present in the training population.

My working hypothesis: self-play training (even with GreedyPolicy mixed in at 10-25%) mostly optimizes the policy to beat the CURRENT SELF-PLAY POPULATION (latest snapshot, pool), and that objective is diluted enough that the gradient signal specifically for beating GreedyPolicy is too weak, even though GreedyPolicy is a *deterministic, non-adaptive, fully exploitable* fixed policy that should in principle be an easy target for a dedicated best-response training run (no distribution shift, perfectly predictable opponent). I am considering: after the current self-play run, do a DEDICATED fine-tuning phase where the training opponent mix is 100% (or close to it) GreedyPolicy -- both opponent seats always GreedyPolicy -- to directly optimize a best response against this exact fixed policy, rather than continuing to dilute the objective across a mixed self-play population.
